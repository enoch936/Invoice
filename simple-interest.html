Simple interest calculator

Simple interest is charged only on the original principal — it never compounds. Use it for short-term loans and flat-rate savings.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

I = P × r × t

Worked example

  1. P = $5,000, r = 6% = 0.06, t = 3 years.
  2. I = 5000 × 0.06 × 3 = $900.
  3. Final balance = $5,000 + $900 = $5,900.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The rate is fixed and the term is exact.
  • Interest is not reinvested — that is what makes it simple.

Frequently asked questions

Simple vs compound interest?

Simple interest is always calculated on the original principal. Compound interest is calculated on the balance, so it grows faster over time.

Where is simple interest used?

Short-term loans, some car finance, flat-rate savings products and legal judgments often use simple interest.
