Markup calculator

Markup and margin both describe profit, but markup is measured against cost while margin is measured against the selling price. This shows both.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

Markup % = (Price − Cost) / Cost × 100; Margin % = (Price − Cost) / Price × 100

Worked example

  1. A product costs $40 and sells for $100.
  2. Profit = $100 − $40 = $60; markup = 60 / 40 = 150%.
  3. Margin = 60 / 100 = 60%, so a 150% markup is only a 60% margin.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Only the unit cost is considered — fees, shipping and taxes are excluded.
  • Both figures are for a single unit.
  • The selling price actually covers the cost.

Frequently asked questions

Why is markup higher than margin?

Markup divides profit by the smaller cost figure, while margin divides it by the larger price, so markup is always the bigger number.

Can I just double the cost?

Doubling the cost is a 100% markup, which is a 50% margin. That may not be enough to cover overheads.

Which should I quote to a supplier?

Buyers usually speak in margin and sellers in markup — always check which one is meant to avoid a nasty surprise.
