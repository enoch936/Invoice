Inventory turnover calculator

Inventory turnover shows how efficiently stock is sold and replaced. Higher turnover usually means less cash tied up in the warehouse.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

Inventory turnover = COGS / Average inventory; Days = 365 / Turnover

Worked example

  1. Annual COGS $600,000 with an average inventory of $100,000.
  2. Turnover = 600000 / 100000 = 6 times a year.
  3. Days = 365 / 6 = 60.8 days of stock on hand.

Assumptions and limitations

  • COGS and inventory levels are for the same period.
  • Inventory is valued consistently at cost.
  • Seasonality is averaged out over the year.

Frequently asked questions

Is a higher turnover always better?

Usually, because it frees cash, but too high can mean stockouts and lost sales — it depends on your margins and lead times.

Why use average inventory?

Inventory swings through the year, so the average start-and-end value better represents the level actually held.

What counts as good turnover?

It varies by industry: grocery can turn 15+ times, while a jewellery or furniture business might turn only twice.
