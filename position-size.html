Position size calculator

Position sizing keeps any single loss small. Enter your balance, the percentage you are willing to risk and the stop distance to see how many lots to trade.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

Lots = (balance × risk%) / (stop in pips × pip value per lot)

Worked example

  1. Balance $10,000, risking 1%, stop 25 pips, pip value $10 per lot.
  2. Risk amount = 10,000 × 1% = $100; risk per lot = 25 × $10 = $250.
  3. Lots = 100 / 250 = 0.40 lots, or about 40,000 units.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The pip value per lot is constant and already known for the pair and lot size you trade.
  • Both entry and stop are assumed to fill at the exact prices given; real fills can slip, especially in fast markets.
  • Spread, commission, swap/funding and slippage are excluded from the risk estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Why risk only a small percentage?

Small, consistent risk keeps a losing streak survivable. Losing 1% ten times in a row costs about 10% of the account; risking 10% each time would nearly wipe it out.

How do I find the pip value per lot?

Use the forex pip value calculator for the pair, or check your broker's contract specification.

Should I round the size down?

Yes — rounding down keeps the risk at or below your limit. Rounding up quietly increases the amount at risk.
