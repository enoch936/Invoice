Market cap and FDV calculator

Market cap values only the coins in circulation; FDV values the maximum supply. Comparing the two shows how much future supply could dilute the price.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

Market cap = price × circulating supply; FDV = price × total (or max) supply

Worked example

  1. Price $2, circulating supply 100,000,000, total supply 200,000,000.
  2. Market cap = 2 × 100,000,000 = $200,000,000.
  3. FDV = 2 × 200,000,000 = $400,000,000, so only 50% of the supply is circulating.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Price and supply figures are a single snapshot and change as coins are mined, burned, unlocked or reserved.
  • Market cap is a notional value, not cash: it multiplies the price by supply rather than what buyers actually paid.
  • Uncapped tokens have no meaningful max-supply FDV, so that field is optional.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between market cap and FDV?

Market cap uses circulating supply; FDV uses total or maximum supply. FDV is therefore always at least as large and shows the cost of valuing every eventual coin.

Why does FDV matter?

A low market cap with a high FDV means a lot of supply has yet to be released. Those unlocks can add selling pressure, so the fully diluted figure is the more cautious valuation.

Is market cap the money invested?

No. It is the price multiplied by supply. Only a small fraction of the coins may actually have traded at that price.
