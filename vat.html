VAT calculator

VAT is a consumption tax added to the net price. Switch between adding it to a price and removing it from a tax-inclusive total.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

Add: gross = net × (1 + rate). Remove: net = gross / (1 + rate)

Worked example

  1. A net price of $1,000 at 20% VAT.
  2. VAT = 1000 × 0.20 = $200.
  3. Gross = $1,200. Removing VAT from $1,200 gives net $1,000 again.

Assumptions and limitations

  • A single VAT rate applies to the whole amount.
  • No zero-rated, reduced or exempt items are mixed in.
  • Rounding is applied only for display.

Frequently asked questions

Why isn't removing VAT just dividing by the rate?

VAT is charged on the net amount, but a gross figure already includes it, so you divide by (1 + rate), not by the rate.

What rate should I use?

Use the rate for your country and product category — standard rates range widely and some goods are reduced or zero-rated.

Is VAT the same as sales tax?

They are similar, but VAT is collected at each stage of the supply chain while sales tax is usually charged once at the final sale.
