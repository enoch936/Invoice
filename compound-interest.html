Compound interest calculator

Compound interest pays interest on your interest. Enter a starting balance, any regular contributions and a rate to see the projected future value.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

FV = P(1 + r/n)^(n t) + PMT × [((1 + r/n)^(n t) − 1) / (r/n)]

Worked example

  1. Start with $1,000, add $100 a month, 5% a year compounded monthly, for 10 years.
  2. r/n = 0.05 / 12 = 0.0041667 and n·t = 120 periods.
  3. The lump sum grows to 1000 × 1.0041667^120 = $1,647.01.
  4. The contributions add a future-value annuity worth about $15,528, giving roughly $17,175 in total.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The rate stays constant for the whole term — real rates move.
  • Contributions are level and made at the time you selected.
  • No tax, fees or inflation are deducted; the figure is a gross projection.

Frequently asked questions

What does compounding frequency change?

More frequent compounding means interest is added (and then earns interest) more often, so the same nominal rate produces a slightly larger result.

Is the result guaranteed?

No. It is a mathematical projection of a constant rate. Real investment returns vary and can be negative.

What is the difference between the two contribution timings?

A start-of-period contribution earns interest in the period it is paid; an end-of-period one does not.
