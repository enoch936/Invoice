Permutations and combinations calculator

Both tools count selections of r items from n. Permutations count order, combinations do not. Choose which one your problem needs.

Formula

P(n, r) = n! / (n − r)! · C(n, r) = n! / (r! × (n − r)!)

Worked example

  1. Choose 2 from 5 where order matters: P(5, 2) = 5! / 3! = 120 / 6 = 20.
  2. Ignore order: C(5, 2) = 5! / (2! × 3!) = 120 / 12 = 10.
  3. Every combination corresponds to 2! = 2 orderings, which is why the results differ by exactly that factor.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Items are all distinct and each can be used at most once (no repetition).
  • n and r are treated as non-negative whole numbers; r must not exceed n.

Frequently asked questions

Permutations or combinations?

Use permutations when the order matters, such as finishing places in a race. Use combinations when it does not, such as choosing lottery numbers.

Why is C(n, r) always smaller than P(n, r)?

Combinations collapse every ordering of the same r items into one group, so they divide out the r! arrangements.

What is 0! ?

By definition 0! = 1, which makes the formulas work when you choose nothing or everything.
