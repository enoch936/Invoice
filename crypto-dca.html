Crypto DCA (dollar-cost averaging) calculator

Dollar-cost averaging spreads a fixed amount across several prices. Enter them to see your average cost and, if you like, the profit or loss at today's price.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

Units bought = amount ÷ price at each buy; average cost = total invested ÷ total units

Worked example

  1. Invest $100 at $10,000, $11,000 and $12,000.
  2. Units = 0.01 + 0.009091 + 0.008333 = 0.027424; total invested = $300.
  3. Average cost = 300 / 0.027424 = $10,939.39; at a current price of $13,000 the position is worth $356.52 (+18.8%).

Assumptions and limitations

  • The same cash amount is invested at each listed price.
  • Buy prices are filled exactly; exchange fees, spread and slippage are excluded.
  • A lower average cost is not a forecast — averaging down does not remove the risk of further losses.

Frequently asked questions

How does DCA lower my average cost?

Because you buy more units when the price is low and fewer when it is high, the average price you pay is below the simple average of the buy prices.

Is dollar-cost averaging safer?

It reduces the risk of buying everything at a single bad price, but it does not protect against a market that keeps falling. You can still lose money.

What about fees on every buy?

Each buy usually pays a trading fee. Many small buys mean more fees, so net returns are slightly lower than shown here.
