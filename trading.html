How the maths works

Position size = (equity × risk%) ÷ (|entry − stop| + round-trip fees per unit). Risking a fixed small percentage keeps one loss from defining your account.

Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward:risk). At 2:1 you break even winning 33.3% of trades before costs.

Expectancy (R) = win rate × reward:risk − loss rate. Kelly = p − (1 − p) ÷ b. Kelly is highly sensitive to estimation error, so many traders use a fraction of it.

Liquidation (isolated) ≈ entry × (1 − 1/leverage + maintenance margin) for longs, and entry × (1 + 1/leverage − maintenance margin) for shorts.

The Monte Carlo uses a seeded random number generator, so the same inputs always give the same picture. It is a model of the numbers you give it — use the LIVE MARKET DATA panel above to load a win rate and reward:risk measured from real candles instead of guessing.

Frequently asked questions

Does this use live market data?

Yes, if you want it to. The LIVE MARKET DATA panel pulls real candles from Binance's public API, shows the current price, ATR and realised volatility, and measures the actual win rate and reward:risk of a simple EMA-crossover rule over the last 500 hourly candles — then you can load those real numbers straight into the tools. Nothing is pre-filled or simulated, and if the feed is unavailable the page says so rather than showing substitute data.

How do I calculate position size?

Decide the percentage of your account you will risk (often 0.5% to 2%), then divide that money by the distance between entry and stop-loss. The result is the number of units to buy or sell.

What is a good risk per trade?

There is no universal figure. Many traders keep risk per trade between 0.5% and 2% of equity so that a long losing streak does not cause a deep drawdown. Use the Monte Carlo tool to see the effect of your own numbers.

What is the break-even win rate?

It is the share of trades you must win to cover your losses, equal to 1 divided by (1 plus reward:risk), before fees. A 1:1 strategy needs more than 50%; a 3:1 strategy needs more than 25%.

Is the Kelly criterion safe to use?

Kelly maximises long-run growth only if your win rate and payoff are exactly right. Real estimates are noisy, so full Kelly can cause very large drawdowns; fractional Kelly is a more conservative choice.

How accurate is the liquidation price?

It is an approximation. Exchanges use their own maintenance margin tiers, fees and mark prices. Always confirm the liquidation price on the platform before opening a leveraged position.

Do these tools give trade signals?

No. They help you size and stress-test risk. Nothing here predicts price direction or guarantees profit.
