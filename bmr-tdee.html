BMR and TDEE calculator (daily calories)

BMR is the energy you would burn at complete rest; TDEE adds your daily activity. Together they estimate calorie needs.

Formula

Mifflin-St Jeor: BMR = 10·kg + 6.25·cm − 5·age + (male ? +5 : −161) · TDEE = BMR × activity factor

Worked example

  1. Male, 70 kg, 175 cm, 30 years, moderately active.
  2. BMR = 700 + 1093.75 − 150 + 5 = 1648.75 kcal.
  3. TDEE = 1648.75 × 1.55 ≈ 2556 kcal a day.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, a common clinical estimate.
  • Activity multipliers are averages and can be off by a few hundred calories.
  • General estimate only — not medical advice; individual needs vary.

Frequently asked questions

BMR versus TDEE?

BMR is the energy to stay alive at rest. TDEE is BMR multiplied by an activity factor, giving what you actually burn in a day.

How accurate is this?

It is a good starting estimate but individual metabolism varies. Track weight over a few weeks and adjust.

Why is there a 5 / −161 difference?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation uses a different constant for males and females to account for average differences in body composition.
