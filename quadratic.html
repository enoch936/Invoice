Quadratic equation solver

A quadratic equation ax² + bx + c = 0 has roots given by the quadratic formula. Enter the coefficients to see the roots and the shape of the curve.

Formula

x = ( −b ± √(b² − 4ac) ) / 2a · discriminant D = b² − 4ac · vertex x = −b / 2a

Worked example

  1. Solve x² − 3x + 2 = 0, so a = 1, b = −3, c = 2.
  2. Discriminant = (−3)² − 4 × 1 × 2 = 9 − 8 = 1, which is positive.
  3. Roots: x = (3 ± 1) / 2 = 2 and 1; the vertex sits at x = 1.5, y = −0.25.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Coefficients are real numbers, so a negative discriminant produces a complex-conjugate pair.
  • a must be non-zero, otherwise the equation is linear rather than quadratic.

Frequently asked questions

What does the discriminant tell me?

It decides the roots: positive means two real roots, zero means one repeated root, and negative means a complex-conjugate pair.

What is the vertex?

The turning point of the parabola. It lies on the axis of symmetry x = −b / 2a, halfway between the real roots when they exist.

Why can a not be zero?

If a is zero the x² term vanishes and the equation becomes linear, with a single root instead of the quadratic behaviour.
