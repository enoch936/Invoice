Risk-reward ratio calculator

The distance to your stop is the risk; the distance to your target is the reward. This calculator turns those prices into a ratio and the win rate you need to break even.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

Reward-to-risk = |target − entry| / |entry − stop|

Worked example

  1. Long: entry 100, stop 95, target 115.
  2. Risk = 5, reward = 15, so the reward-to-risk ratio is 3.0.
  3. Break-even win rate = 1 / (1 + 3) = 25%; a $10,000 account risking 1% puts $100 at risk for a $300 target.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Only the entry, stop and target prices drive the ratio; position size is assumed to match the risk percentage you set.
  • Spread, commission, swap/funding and slippage are excluded, and both stop and target are assumed to fill at the exact price.
  • It assumes you exit at either the stop or the target, not somewhere in between.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good risk-reward ratio?

There is no single answer, but higher ratios tolerate lower win rates. At 1:2 you need to win over 33%; at 1:3, over 25%.

Does a high ratio guarantee profit?

No. A high ratio only helps if your targets are actually reached. A strategy that rarely hits its target can still lose money.

How does the break-even win rate work?

It is the win rate at which winnings exactly match losses: 1 / (1 + ratio). Fees and slippage push the real requirement higher.
