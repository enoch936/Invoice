Profit margin calculator

Profit margin shows what share of every sale you keep. Enter revenue, the cost of goods sold and your operating expenses to see both gross and net margins.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

Gross margin = (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue × 100; Net margin = (Revenue − COGS − OpEx) / Revenue × 100

Worked example

  1. Revenue $100,000, COGS $55,000, operating expenses $25,000.
  2. Gross profit = 100000 − 55000 = $45,000, so gross margin = 45%.
  3. Net profit = 100,000 − 55,000 − 25,000 = $20,000, a 20% net margin.

Assumptions and limitations

  • COGS contains only costs that scale directly with sales.
  • Operating expenses are fixed for the period shown.
  • Tax and interest are not deducted — this is an operating profit.

Frequently asked questions

Gross margin or net margin?

Gross margin measures the profitability of what you sell; net margin also absorbs overheads, so it shows what the business actually keeps.

What is a good profit margin?

It varies widely by industry. Software and services often run 60–80% gross margins, while retail and distribution run much thinner.

Why is my net margin negative?

Operating expenses exceed the gross profit, so the business is losing money on its current sales volume.
