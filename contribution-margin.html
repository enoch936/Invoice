Contribution margin calculator

Contribution margin is what each sale leaves behind to pay fixed costs and profit. It is the building block of break-even and pricing decisions.

Estimates only. Results depend on the inputs you provide and the assumptions listed below. This is an educational tool, not financial, tax or investment advice.

Formula

Contribution margin % = (Price − Variable cost) / Price × 100

Worked example

  1. Price $50, variable cost $30, fixed costs $10,000.
  2. Contribution = $20 per unit; contribution margin = 20 / 50 = 40%.
  3. Break-even = 10000 / 20 = 500 units.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Variable cost is strictly per unit and does not change with volume.
  • Fixed costs are constant.
  • Price is the net price received before discounts.

Frequently asked questions

Contribution margin vs gross margin?

Contribution margin deducts only variable costs, while gross margin usually deducts all product costs including some fixed overhead.

What is a good contribution margin?

Software and services often exceed 70%; physical retail can be under 30%. The key is whether it covers fixed costs.

Why does contribution matter for pricing?

Discounting a low-margin product can wipe out the contribution entirely, so it shows the floor price you can accept.
